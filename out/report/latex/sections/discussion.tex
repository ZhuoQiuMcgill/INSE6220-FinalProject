\subsection{Dimensionality Reduction vs. Predictive Power}
Our analysis demonstrates a clear trade-off between model simplicity and predictive accuracy. While the first four principal components capture nearly 82\% of the variance in the feature space, models trained on this reduced set consistently underperformed those trained on the full 12-dimensional dataset. This suggests that the "minor" components (PC5-PC12), which account for the remaining 18\% of variance, contain subtle but discriminative information essential for distinguishing difficult borderline cases. PCA, being an unsupervised technique, maximizes variance rather than class separability; thus, the directions of maximum variation are not necessarily the directions of maximum classification margin.

\subsection{Model Performance and Complexity}
The comparison between Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and MLP highlights the non-linear nature of the KOI vetting problem. The linear baseline (Logistic Regression) plateaued at an accuracy of $\approx 80\%$, failing to capture complex interactions between features like transit duration, depth, and stellar density. In contrast, the Random Forest and MLP models achieved accuracies near 88-89\%. The MLP's superior performance (F1=0.89) indicates that a smooth, continuous non-linear function is well-suited to approximate the probabilistic nature of \texttt{koi\_score}.

\subsection{Astrophysical Interpretation}
The feature importance analysis reinforces standard exoplanet detection theory. Planetary radius (\texttt{koi\_prad}) and transit depth (\texttt{koi\_depth}) emerged as the most influential predictors. This is intuitive: larger planets block more light, creating deeper and more significant signals that are less likely to be confused with noise or instrumental artifacts. The prominence of signal-to-noise ratio (\texttt{koi\_model\_snr}) further supports this, as high-SNR events are inherently more reliable.

\subsection{Limitations and Discrepancies}
An anomaly observed in our results is the discrepancy between the high cross-validation AUC scores ($\approx 0.95$ for MLP/RF) and the ROC curves generated on the test set, which appeared closer to random guessing in the provided visualizations. This likely stems from an issue in the probability extraction or plotting code rather than the models themselves, given that the accuracy, precision, and recall metrics remain robustly high. Future work should address this calibration issue. Additionally, our reliance on catalog-level summary statistics ignores the rich time-series information in the raw light curves, which the actual Robovetter uses; incorporating such data could further bridge the gap between our surrogate model and the full pipeline.
