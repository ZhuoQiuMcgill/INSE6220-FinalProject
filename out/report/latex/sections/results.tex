\subsection{Principal Component Analysis}
The eigenvalue decomposition of the standardized feature covariance matrix reveals that the effective dimensionality of the data is significantly lower than 12. As shown in Table \ref{tab:pca_variance}, the first three principal components (PCs) explain approximately 73.5\% of the total variance, and the first four explain approximately 81.8\%. This justifies our choice of $k=4$ for the reduced dimensionality experiments, as we retain over 80\% of the signal while reducing the feature count by two-thirds.

The decision boundaries in the PC1-PC2 plane (Figure \ref{fig:decision_boundaries}) visualize how the models separate the classes in the latent space. Feature importance analysis from the Random Forest model (Figure \ref{fig:feat_imp}) confirms that planetary radius (\texttt{koi\_prad}) is the most critical predictor (importance $\approx 0.20$), followed by transit depth (\texttt{koi\_depth}) and period (\texttt{koi\_period}).
